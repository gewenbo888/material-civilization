# Material Civilization · 物质文明

A bilingual (EN / 中文) systems-level platform reading civilization as a materials-science problem. Built around the thesis that civilization is the history of matter becoming programmable — that empires, energy systems, computation, architecture, warfare, and even biology are all constrained by what humans can extract, refine, and shape, and at what precision and cost. The throughline is one trajectory: matter becoming, slowly and at increasing precision, programmable.

The site is single-page and includes a nine-age material-civilization timeline with full breakthrough + civilizational-consequence framing for each (Stone → Copper → Bronze → Iron → Steel → Oil & Plastic → Semiconductor → AI Chip → Quantum Materials), each row colour-coded by its canonical material; an animated SVG scale-ladder visualization stepping from atom (~0.1 nm) to molecule to crystal lattice to defect to microstructure to bulk (~1 m) — ten orders of magnitude visualized as a single connected diagram with a vacancy and a dopant rendered explicitly; a six-card materials-and-empires module (Bronze charioteers → Iron republics → Coal-steam empire → Steel-and-railway state → Oil-fuelled superpower → Semiconductor hegemony); a seven-row energy-density table that crosses seven orders of magnitude (wood ≈ 16 MJ/kg → uranium-235 ≈ 80,000,000 MJ/kg) with civilizational role per material; a six-card silicon-civilization deep dive (element & ore → ultra-purity → single crystal → lithography → doping → packaging); a four-card materials-and-the-human-body module (calcium hydroxyapatite, iron in haemoglobin, carbon backbone, neural conductivity); a six-card architecture-and-megastructures module from megalithic stone to carbon-fibre composite; a six-card warfare-and-materials module from bronze edge to composite drone; a six-dial material-civilization simulator (extraction, energy density, manufacturing precision, information processing, structural complexity, coordination scale → capability, frontier, leverage, fragility) that maps any configuration onto a recognisable archetype (frontier-industrial, heavy-industrial, post-industrial design economy, resource-export trap, cognitive-civilization frontier, or high-fragility); a six-card future-materials module (graphene, room-temperature superconductors, metamaterial optics, programmable matter, molecular manufacturing, bio-fabricated structural) with explicit timeline-uncertainty caveats; a four-card material-philosophy module (matter ↔ information, entropy as constraint, computation as material process, self-organising matter); and a five-question Q&A panel that answers structurally without elitism — bronze-to-iron transition, semiconductors-as-oil, whether a single material defines the next era, why materials science is slower than software, and whether civilisation can fatigue like a material.

## Stack

Plain HTML, CSS, vanilla JS. Inline SVG for the atomic-to-bulk scale ladder (with all six panels rendered: an atom with electron orbitals, a three-atom molecule with covalent bonds, a 3×3 crystal lattice, a defective lattice with a vacancy and a substitutional dopant, a microstructural grain-boundary patch, and a bulk skyscraper outline). Space Grotesk display + Source Serif 4 body + Noto Serif SC Chinese + JetBrains Mono. Industrial-laboratory palette: dark graphite background, copper primary, iron-rust, crystal cyan, uranium yellow, silicon violet, carbon graphite — colour codes mapped semantically across every section so the same material reads the same colour everywhere.
